Network automation that leaves the engineer free.

An NMS decides what you can automate, and when. This method is not another NMS: you decide, one problem at a time, starting from the configuration already on your devices, with what you learn kept in packages you own and release.

Why

Two problems you know

What automation configures does not stay

Configuration drifts. Scripts push once and walk away, and nothing tells you whether what they sent still holds.

What you know has nowhere to live

What makes the network work is what you know. The setting the YANG does not model. The write the router refuses while another is in progress. The new key that does nothing until the SA is cleared. All of it lives only in your head, a wiki or a post-incident note, and you are the one who has to remember it, every time.

The answer on offer to both is an NMS: the vendor automates what it chose to support, encodes what network engineers know, and sells it back to you, late.

What follows has nothing to buy and no platform to move onto: packages you install, read, change and release yourself, on plain Kubernetes and Crossplane.

Why

Computing solved this years ago

How computing does it

Write down what should be there, let a control loop keep it there, and change it through Git. Servers, cloud and applications have run this way for more than a decade. What a human operator knows went into software that does the work.

Why networks were left out

A router is not a container. You cannot throw it away and start a fresh one. It holds a configuration somebody wrote by hand, and its vendor decides how it can be reached.

How

The method: the same loop, for devices as they are

Each section of a device's configuration becomes an object. Two loops meet on it.

  • You change the spec through Git, as a pull request. Review, history and rollback come with it.
  • Crossplane reads the router into the status on every poll and writes back what differs. A change made by hand is put back.
  • The status says whether it works, not only what was sent: the tunnel's interface, its SA, the BGP session. A next step can wait on it.
  • Try every change on a twin first: the same objects against a lab of the same devices, on your laptop.

Both loops on one real object, a tunnel interface on Cisco IOS XE, shortened:

kind: TunnelInterface
metadata:
  name: r1--tunnel0--d311cfe5
spec:                            # what should be there: yours, through Git
  managementPolicy: Replace
  deviceRef: {name: r1}
  name: Tunnel0
  mode: ipsec-ipv4
  ipv4Address: 192.168.100.1/24
  source: GigabitEthernet2
  destination: 10.0.23.2
  ipsecProfile: CLASSICAL-IPSEC
status:                          # read from the router on every poll
  observed:                      # the section as the router holds it
    destination: 10.0.23.2
    ipsecProfile: CLASSICAL-IPSEC
    ipv4Address: 192.168.100.1/24
    mode: ipsec-ipv4
    source: GigabitEthernet2
  drift: []                      # nothing differs from the spec
  oper:                          # whether it works
    interface: ready
    peers:
    - address: 10.0.23.2
      ikeSA: active
  reverted:                      # a description added by hand, taken off
    extra:
    - field: description
      device: ['"by hand"']

Observe before writing

A router configured by hand is taken over one section at a time. Nothing is written until you move a section up.

Each step is one field of the object above, managementPolicy, changed in a pull request. Without it, an object only reads. Replace need not be the goal: Merge keeps your intent and leaves the rest of the section as it is, and a section can stay there. A router adopted, step by step

How

Kubernetes, in network terms

To start, Kubernetes on your laptop is enough. Most of it maps onto things you already know:

On the networkIn Kubernetes
the intended configurationan object's spec
show commandsthe object's status
a routing protocol, convergingreconcile: the loop that keeps converging on the spec
the CLIkubectl
a configuration template you adda new kind of object, defined in YAML with Crossplane

Kubernetes runs in Docker, with kind (Kubernetes in Docker). For convenience, one command does the setup:

uvx netclab up

It runs the steps you could run by hand: a kind cluster, then Crossplane and a lab when asked. What it leaves is a plain cluster, and kubectl works with it as usual. Images such as cEOS you bring yourself, as for any lab.

How

Your knowledge is the asset

What you learn about a device goes into a package you own, tested against the device and released. It stops being something in your head and becomes something your team versions, reviews and builds on. Nobody waits for a vendor's roadmap, and the next engineer installs a version instead of finding it out again.

What an engineer found outWhat the package does about it
IOS XE 17.18 has no post-quantum preshared key in its YANGwrites it over the CLI by RPC; on 26.x, where the YANG has it, over RESTCONF, picked by the router's version
IOS XE refuses overlapping writes (409 lock-denied)each object waits until the objects it names are Ready
a key changed without a clear leaves the SA on the old keyevery rotation writes the key, then clears the peer's SA
AWS's tunnel telemetry lags the router by minutesthe status columns read the router
new keys on both tunnels at once take both tunnels downrotates one tunnel at a time; the next once the first is up on its new key, with BGP established

Packages go to a public registry or to your own private one. What your team learns about your network can stay yours.

Your turn

Build your own

A new kind of object is two pieces of YAML, with no controller code:

If you have written Jinja templates for Ansible, the composition will look familiar.

Templates go a long way: configuration-iosxe rotates its keys with templates alone. When a kind decides more, the decisions can go into a function: configuration-aws-vpn runs function-aws-vpn, written in Python, before its templates. Which way is yours to pick; Crossplane has function SDKs for Go and Python.

Floor by floor

A kind can compose other kinds. Each floor is a package with its own releases, and there are as many floors as the problem needs.

A VPN between two IOS XE routers needs nothing above configuration-iosxe. A VPN to AWS adds a floor: the VPN team works with the few fields of a Connection, and what the IOS XE expert encoded below is used without being repeated.

Where a good model already exists, adopt it instead of building one. An AVD repository keeps Arista AVD's own design model, the knowledge its community encoded, and the cluster runs it as it is.

Start with the exercises

It started with Arista EOS: configuration-eos holds the first exercises, settings over eAPI and RESTCONF, rough in places. It is being rewritten as a tutorial, a step a page: a device, then a BGP neighbor between two cEOS nodes, with the session's state in the status.

What stays your decision

  • which problem to solve first
  • how fast and how far: each section observed first, then merged or replaced
  • how a device is reached: RESTCONF, eAPI, or its CLI where the YANG stops
  • when: there is no roadmap to wait for
  • whether to build a model, build on one, or adopt one that exists
  • where your packages live: a public registry, or a private one
  • whether to leave: plain Kubernetes and Crossplane, open source under Apache-2.0
